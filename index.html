A few things worth knowing

This is a planning tool, not financial advice. Investment returns are uncertain; mortgage interest saved is based on the rates you enter. Keep an emergency fund and consider access to your money before overpaying.

How the calculations work

Mortgage payments. A repayment mortgage with monthly interest at the annual rate divided by 12. The variable rate stays constant after the fixed period. At that change, we recalculate the baseline payment over the remaining term and use it in both plans, so overpayments reduce the term, not the monthly payment. A lender may instead reduce your payment.

Timing and allowances. Today's lump sum is paid before any interest. Monthly and future lump sums are paid at month end, after interest and the regular payment. Year 1's allowance uses the balance before today's lump sum. Limits trigger alerts; we do not cap payments or calculate early repayment charges. The fixed-period allowance is not prorated on expiry.

Investment comparison. Growth compounds monthly using (1 + annual return)^(1/12) − 1. Contributions arrive at month end. Both plans retain any starting investment balance. In the overpayment plan, surplus cash and payments freed by early payoff are invested at the same return. Contributions continue to your chosen horizon, including scheduled lump sums after payoff.

Annual withdrawals. The third strategy uses the same contribution amounts, growth rate and horizon as the investment strategy. New contributions and their growth are withdrawn every 12 months from today, after that month's interest, regular mortgage payment and investment contribution. Only the amount needed to clear the remaining debt is withdrawn. Existing starting investments stay invested separately; after payoff, new contributions and freed mortgage payments stay invested too.

Annual strategy limits and timing. Actual withdrawals, including any investment gains, count against the mortgage allowance in the year and rate period when they are paid, not when money was invested. No additional withdrawal is made at a shorter comparison horizon or partial final year. Mortgage savings and payoff timing cover the full mortgage term; net-position comparisons use the selected horizon. Withdrawal taxes, fees and market volatility are not modelled.

What isn't included. No separate taxes, investment fees, inflation, lender fees, daily interest, or repayment penalties are calculated. Enter an investment return net of tax and fees. Currency changes labels only, not exchange rates. Calculations use full precision, with displayed figures rounded; your lender's results will differ.
